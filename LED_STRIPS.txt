LEAKCAM illumination: LED strip boards
=====================================

Replaces the earlier ring-board plan (LED_RING.md). Four small strip boards, two per camera,
panelled with the main board and cut apart by hand. White and IR are two separate chains so they
can be switched and dimmed independently.


1. Topology
-----------

  4 strips x (2 white + 2 IR) = 8 white + 8 IR.

  White chain: U14 (TPS61160) -> J1 -> strip A -> B (camera 1) -> C -> D (camera 2) -> J1
  IR chain:    U15 (TPS61160) -> J2 -> strip A -> B (camera 1) -> C -> D (camera 2) -> J2

  Each strip carries both chains: 4 wire pads, one "in" pair at one end (W+, IR+), one "out" pair
  at the other end (W-, IR-). The return wire of each chain runs back beside the forward wires;
  twist the pairs on every hop (keeps loops small, see RF below).

  One open LED darkens its whole chain on both cameras; the other chain keeps working.


2. Electrical (set on the main board, LEDS.SchDoc)
--------------------------------------------------

  Chain  Driver  CTRL / PWM         R_FB             I_LED    String V (typ / max)  Switch peak (max)
  -----  ------  -----------------  ---------------  -------  --------------------  -----------------
  white  U14,J1  GPIO61 PWM1 CTL2   R66 6.8R C23214  29.4 mA  ~21 V / 27.2 V        ~0.54 A
  IR     U15,J2  GPIO60 PWM0 CTL1   R68 5.6R C322191 35.7 mA  ~11 V / 19.2 V        ~0.49 A

  - I_LED = 0.2 V / R_FB at 100 % PWM duty; the TPS61160 turns duty into DC current (no flicker
    or rolling-shutter banding).
  - Limits: OVP 37 V min, DSK24 40 V, C1/C104 50 V, switch current limit 0.56 A min, Dmax 93 %.
  - Switch peak model: 3.0 V battery, L = 10 uH, 600 kHz, 85 % efficiency. "Max" uses the LEDs'
    datasheet Vf max, which is specified at 350 mA; at 30-36 mA the real Vf is lower.
  - Why R66 is 6.8R and not 5.6R: eight white LEDs at 35.7 mA can reach ~0.61 A switch peak on
    a low battery and hit the current limit (not damaging: the light just comes out dimmer than
    set). 6.8R keeps the white chain inside the limit.
  - Option, not applied: R68 3.3R (IR 60 mA, ~+70 % IR light). IR chain worst case then
    ~12.8 V, switch peak ~0.51 A: still inside the limit. Decide after the bench test (section 8).

4. Footprint (one footprint for both LEDs)
------------------------------------------

  Pad roles differ per part:
    JNJ white   pad 1 cathode, pad 2 anode, centre pad 3 = anode
    JNJ IR      pad 1 cathode, pad 2 anode, centre pad 3 = not connected
    Xinglight white: outer pads cathode / anode, centre = thermal
    Xinglight IR:    centre pad 2 = anode, pad 1 anode, pad 3 cathode
  Shared footprint: left outer pad = cathode, right outer pad = anode, centre pad tied to the
  anode net. Pad widths per the JNJ recommended layout: 1.0 / 0.6 / 0.5 mm, 3.3 mm long.
  Silkscreen a cathode mark; makers mark polarity differently (dot, notch, corner cut), so check
  the first placed part of each type against its datasheet.


5. Mechanical
-------------

  Strip: 36 x 6 mm, same stack as the main board (panelled). One screw, centred at x = 18 mm.
    - M2: 2.2 mm non-plated hole, no copper ring; head keep-out 4.5 mm diameter (M1.6: 1.8 mm
      hole, 3.0 mm head).
    - LED order along the strip: W - IR - (screw) - IR - W, centres at x = 4.5, 11, 25, 31.5 mm,
      so both colours are centred on the same spot.
    - Wire pads at the strip ends, about 1.5 x 2 mm each, two per end.
    - The two strings pass the screw on an inner layer (insulated under a metal screw head);
      0.5 mm from the hole wall.
  Anti-rotation: one screw lets the strip turn. Print a 6.2 mm pocket or ribs that the strip sits
  in; the same boss is wedged to give the tilt (section 6). Alternative: a 1.0 mm hole ~8 mm
  from the screw for a moulded pin.
  Screw into plastic: M2 heat-set insert, or an M2 thread-forming screw directly into PLA/PETG.

  Printed cover:
    - Press the strip flat against the inside of the wall; wall 1.0 mm thick at the LEDs, one
      3.4 mm round hole per LED. The 0.7-0.8 mm LED base sits inside the wall and the dome stands
      about 1.0-1.1 mm proud of the outer surface. The chip ends up ~0.3 mm below the outer
      surface with 1.7 mm hole radius: the wall clips only light beyond ~80 deg.
    - Seal with a bead of clear silicone around each dome.
    - Shell opaque (black or dark). Keep >= 2-3 mm of solid plastic between the LED holes and the
      lens opening: printed layers guide light sideways, and 850 nm passes through thin plastic,
      black included. Line pocket walls facing the lens with black vinyl tape or matte paint if in
      doubt (no metal tape near the antenna).
    - Never put LEDs behind the lens's own window or dome.

  Panel: strips on the sides of the main board inside one rectangle; cut line drawn on silkscreen
  only (no slots or mouse bites, which JLC counts as extra designs). Copper >= 1 mm from the cut
  line on every layer, no trace crosses it. Components >= 2.5 mm from the outer panel edge, or
  JLC adds rails.


6. Glare
--------

  - Direct: the 222 deg lens sees 21 deg behind its front plane. Every LED must sit outside that
    cone (an LED 12 mm off-axis must be > ~4.6 mm behind the front element, plus margin).
  - Specular: tilt the strips 30-45 deg outward from the camera axis so a shiny surface facing the
    camera (wet floor, water) does not return a hotspot into the image centre. With a 120-130 deg
    beam the centre of the scene stays lit at 45 deg tilt.
  - Internal: separate LED openings from the lens opening (see section 5).


7. RF (2.45 GHz Wi-Fi/BLE antenna)
----------------------------------

  - Strips (and their wires) at or below y = 43 mm, the same limit as the camera boards;
    >= 20 mm from the antenna, 30 mm or more preferred.
  - No copper pour on the strips: only the string traces and pads.
  - Strip trace + wire forms a monopole on the main ground: avoid 25-35 mm and 85-100 mm total
    length; 45-75 mm is good. The chains cross both camera sides, so twist go and return wires on
    every hop and route them away from the antenna, not alongside it.
  - Tilting the strips has no RF effect.


8. Brightness estimate (verify on the bench)
--------------------------------------------

  Per camera: 2 strips = 4 white + 4 IR, tilted ~30-45 deg.

  White, 29.4 mA: ~10 lm per LED (flux scaled from 100-150 lm @350 mA), ~3 cd on axis;
  toward the image centre about 10 cd per camera:
      0.5 m ~ 40 lx     1 m ~ 10 lx     2 m ~ 2.5 lx
  IR, 35.7 mA: ~9 mW/sr per LED, ~30 mW/sr per camera toward the centre. With the OV5647's
  ~25 % relative sensitivity at 850 nm this is roughly half the white light as seen by the
  sensor: about 5 "sensor-equivalent" lx at 1 m (about 8 with R68 = 3.3R).

  For change detection at 0.3-2 m this is adequate at 1 m and marginal at 2 m. Levers, in order:
    1. exposure time: 1280x960 runs at 45 fps, so AE is capped near 22 ms; a slower frame rate
       (or 2592x1944 at 10 fps) gives several times more light per frame at little battery cost,
       since the LEDs are only on for the shot;
    2. R68 3.3R for IR (section 2);
    3. more LEDs: not without a third driver (both chains are near the switch current limit).
  Bench test: run leakcam_capture at the expected distances, dark room, -b 100,0 and -b 0,100;
  the "mean_new" field is the frame's mean luminance; aim for >= 40 at the farthest distance.


9. Firmware
-----------

  firmware/k230_capture/leakcam_capture.c: -b white%,ir% (white = PWM1, IR = PWM0), 25 kHz PWM,
  0 leaves a chain off (e.g. -b 0,100 for IR only, no visible flash).
  The IR LEDs are only useful with the cameras' IR-cut filter removed.
